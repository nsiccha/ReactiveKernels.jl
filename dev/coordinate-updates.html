Coordinate updates ​

prepare_coordinate_conditional prepares the conditional log density of one packed coordinate given all others, for coordinate-wise samplers such as slice sampling within Gibbs. The model is written as usual. When its density reads a product X * beta of a constant matrix and a parameter vector, the conditional of beta[j] keeps that product cached: a trial value moves it by one column, X[:, j] * (z - beta[j]), so a trial costs O(n) instead of O(n d). This is the compute-graph Gibbs construction of Luu et al. (2024, arXiv:2410.03630), derived from the emitted program rather than written by hand.

julia
using ReactiveKernelsPPL, Random

logistic_model = @rkppl begin
    beta[axes(X, 2)] .~ Normal.(0, sigma)
    y .~ Bernoulli.(logistic.(X * beta))
end
rng = Xoshiro(1)
X = randn(rng, 40, 5)
y = Int.(rand(rng, 40) .< 0.5)
plan = logistic_model(; X, sigma = 10.0) | (; y)
built = build_kernel(plan)
problem = prepare_coordinate_conditional(built, plan, zeros(5))
state = coordinate_state(problem, zeros(5))
coordinate_logdensity(state, 2, 0.5)

-31.790540672402294

Moving a coordinate ​

coordinate_state(problem, u0) holds a copy of the packed point and its cached products; several states can share one prepared problem. The joint density must be finite at the start.

  • coordinate_logdensity(state, k, z) evaluates the conditional of coordinate k at z and leaves the state unchanged.

  • set_coordinate!(state, k, z) moves coordinate k and updates each cached product by one column.

  • refresh_products!(state) recomputes the products from the point and returns the largest absolute change, the rounding drift of the column updates.

A conditional keeps only the emitted density terms that read the moved coordinate, so it equals the joint log density up to a constant. That constant differs between coordinates: compare values of one coordinate only.

Slice sweeps ​

coordinate_slice_step!(rng, state, k) updates one coordinate by a slice sampling transition of its conditional: stepping out by doubling, shrinkage, and the acceptance test for doubled intervals (Neal 2003). It leaves the conditional, and therefore the joint posterior, invariant. coordinate_slice_sweep!(rng, state) updates every coordinate in order. Both return the number of conditional evaluations.

julia
draws = map(1:500) do _
    coordinate_slice_sweep!(rng, state)
    copy(state.position)
end
(; evaluations_per_sweep = coordinate_slice_sweep!(rng, state),
   posterior_mean = sum(draws[251:end]) / 250,
   drift = refresh_products!(state))

(evaluations_per_sweep = 37, posterior_mean = [-0.139803665613155, -0.03919954519051323, 0.16183656636583577, -0.033856020310911804, 0.008585960096979574], drift = 5.551115123125783e-15)

The defaults are an initial width of 10 and at most 20 doublings, as in the slice sampler of Pigeons.jl. width may also be a vector with one width per coordinate. A sweep typically costs a few evaluations per coordinate.

What is cached ​

  • A product A * w is cached when w is a contiguous run of packed coordinates read whole, as an unconstrained vector parameter is, and A does not depend on any coordinate.

  • A coordinate of such a vector evaluates only the terms that read the vector. A term summing a plate over the vector's cells is read at the moved coordinate's cell alone: the vector's prior, its Jacobian, or a likelihood with one plate cell per element, as in @plate for j in eachindex(y); y[j] .~ Normal.(theta[j], 1); end.

  • A term summing a plate that reads the vector through a bound index vector, as y .~ Normal.(theta[g], 1) does with group labels g, is read only at the rows whose index is the moved coordinate's. A group with no rows reads none. So a coordinate of a group-level vector costs about its own group's rows, whatever the number of groups.

  • A scalar parameter read at a known packed position evaluates the terms that depend on it. With theta[1:J] .~ Normal.(mu, tau) and y .~ Normal.(theta[g], 1), moving mu or tau evaluates its own prior, its Jacobian and the J group priors. It skips every observation row. A scalar used directly in the likelihood still evaluates that likelihood. Unrecognized packed reads use the general conditional.

Not covered yet: models with free integer parameters are refused, and a product over a transformed vector (a positive coefficient vector, for example) is recomputed in full. Execution is native; there is no JAX coordinate sampler.

When it pays ​

Coordinate updates mix well when the coefficients are weakly correlated, and the cached product makes each sweep cost about as much as a few full density evaluations whatever the number of coefficients. On a Gaussian-prior logistic regression of 62 gene-expression samples with 2,000 predictors (the colon data of Luu et al.), slice sweeps reached an effective sample size per second within a factor of two of WarmupHMC's NUTS on the same model, and ahead of it by the paper's median-ESS summary. Many predictors alone are not enough: on a synthetic design with 1,024 predictors and 128 observations, NUTS was about seven times more efficient. With fewer coefficients than observations, or with strongly correlated coefficients, NUTS through prepare_sampler was ten to a hundred times more efficient. Start from NUTS, and measure coordinate updates against it on the model at hand before switching.
